Operational Legacy

Major Operations of the Indian Army

A documented, chronological study of landmark operations launched to defend Indian territory, counter aggression, and uphold international peace under UN mandates.

Historical Clarification: Operations Sharing the Name “Vijay”

In Indian military historiography, the codename Operation Vijay was used on two distinct historical occasions: first in December 1961 for the armed action that liberated Goa, Daman, and Diu from 450 years of Portuguese colonial rule; and second in May–July 1999 for the high-altitude joint operation to evict Pakistani intruders from the Kargil sector of Jammu and Kashmir. Both operations are documented independently below.

Historical Case Studies

Selected Landmark Operations (1947–Present)

Click on any operation to view verified historical background, operational objectives, strategic outcomes, and citations.

Dates: 27 October 1947 – 1 January 1949
Operational Objective: Secure the Kashmir Valley, Jammu, and Ladakh regions against invading tribal raiders supported by the Pakistan Army following Jammu & Kashmir's legal accession to India.
Army's Role: On 27 October 1947, 1st Battalion, Sikh Regiment, commanded by Lieutenant Colonel Dewan Ranjit Rai, was airlifted from Palam to Srinagar airfield, halting the advance of invaders at Baramulla. Indian forces recaptured Uri, Poonch, and Naushera, and secured Ladakh through heroic high-altitude operations across Zoji La Pass using Stuart light tanks.
Outcome: Secured approximately two-thirds of the princely state. A UN-mediated ceasefire took effect at 23:59 hours on 1 January 1949, formalising the Cease Fire Line (CFL).

Reference: Ministry of Defence, Operations in Jammu & Kashmir 1947–48, Government of India.

Dates: 18 – 19 December 1961
Operational Objective: End Portuguese colonial control over Goa, Daman, and Diu, integrating them into the Indian Union after decades of diplomatic failure.
Army's Role: Major General K.P. Candeth commanded the 17th Infantry Division and 50th Parachute Brigade, which moved into Goa in a swift 36-hour multi-pronged operation.
Outcome: Portuguese Governor General Manuel António Vassalo e Silva signed the instrument of surrender on 19 December 1961, concluding 451 years of Portuguese colonial presence in India with minimal casualties.

Reference: Historical Division, Ministry of External Affairs & MoD, The Liberation of Goa.

Dates: August – 23 September 1965
Operational Objective: Neutralise Pakistan's Operation Gibraltar (covert infiltration into Kashmir) and Operation Grand Slam (armoured thrust towards Akhnoor), followed by strategic counter-offensives across the Punjab border.
Army's Role: The Indian Army captured the strategically vital Haji Pir Pass in J&K, preventing infiltration routes. In Punjab, Indian armoured and infantry formations engaged enemy Patton tanks in the Battle of Asal Uttar (creating "Patton Nagar") and the Battle of Phillora.
Outcome: UN Security Council Resolution 211 mandated a ceasefire on 23 September 1965, followed by the signing of the Tashkent Declaration on 10 January 1966.

Reference: Official History of the 1965 Indo-Pak War, Ministry of Defence.

Dates: 3 – 16 December 1971
Operational Objective: Repel pre-emptive Pakistani air and ground strikes on western airfields and execute a decisive combined-arms campaign in East Pakistan to halt genocidal atrocities and liberate Bangladesh.
Army's Role: Guided by Chief of Army Staff General Sam Manekshaw and executed in the East by Lieutenant General Jagjit Singh Aurora, Indian formations bypassed fortified border towns, executed rapid amphibious and airborne river crossings (Meghna Heli-bridge and Tangail airdrop), and advanced to Dacca in 13 days.
Outcome: Complete strategic victory. Pakistan's Eastern Commander Lieutenant General A.A.K. Niazi surrendered on 16 December 1971 in Dacca with 93,000 personnel. The sovereign nation of Bangladesh was established.

Reference: Government of India, Official History of the 1971 War, Natraj Publishers / MoD.

Dates: Launched 13 April 1984 (Ongoing permanent garrison)
Operational Objective: Pre-empt Pakistani plans (codenamed Operation Ababeel) to occupy the undemarcated Saltoro Ridge west of Siachen Glacier, securing strategic passes Bilafond La, Sia La, and Gyong La.
Army's Role: Spearheaded by Lieutenant General P.N. Hoon, troops of the Kumaon Regiment and Ladakh Scouts were airlifted onto the glacier by IAF Mi-17 and Cheetah helicopters in extreme sub-zero conditions (-50°C). Troops secured the heights of the Saltoro Ridge before opposing forces arrived.
Outcome: India established full defensive control over the 76-km Siachen Glacier and the dominating Saltoro Ridge. It remains the highest operational battlefield in the world.

Reference: Ministry of Defence, Parliamentary Standing Committee on Defence Reports on Siachen.

Dates: 23 – 26 June 1987
Operational Objective: Capture the fortified Pakistani post "Quaid" located at 21,153 feet on the Saltoro Ridge, which dominated Indian supply routes on the Bilafond sub-sector.
Army's Role: Named in honour of Second Lieutenant Rajiv Pande who died in an earlier reconnaissance assault. Naib Subedar Bana Singh of 8 JAK Light Infantry led a four-man team scaling a sheer 1,500-foot vertical wall of ice under white-out conditions, lobbed grenades into the fortified bunker, and cleared the post in hand-to-hand combat.
Outcome: Complete capture of the highest post on the Saltoro Ridge, renamed Bana Top in honour of Naib Subedar Bana Singh, who was awarded the Param Vir Chakra.

Reference: Indian Army Gallantry Citations (PVC Citation: Naib Subedar Bana Singh).

Dates: 3 – 5 November 1988
Operational Objective: Thwart an armed mercenary coup attempt against the democratically elected government of President Maumoon Abdul Gayoom in Malé, Maldives, following an emergency request for international assistance.
Army's Role: Within nine hours of receiving the distress appeal, the 50th Independent Parachute Brigade, transported by Indian Air Force Il-76 transport aircraft from Agra, landed non-stop at Hulhule Airport, secured Malé city, and rescued the Maldivian leadership without harming civilian hostages.
Outcome: Coup neutralized within 24 hours with zero Indian casualties; international recognition of India's role as a net security provider and responsible regional democracy.

Reference: Ministry of External Affairs, Operation Cactus in the Maldives, Official Report.

Dates: May – 26 July 1999
Operational Objective: Evict covert Pakistani armed intrusions from fortified high-altitude positions (14,000–18,000 feet) overlooking the Srinagar-Leh National Highway 1D across Dras, Mushkoh, Tololing, Batalik, and Chorbat La.
Army's Role: 8 Mountain Division and 3 Infantry Division, backed by direct fire from 155mm Bofors howitzers and IAF ground-attack aircraft (Operation Safed Sagar), conducted frontal uphill night assaults across sheer rock cliffs. Landmark peaks recaptured include Tololing, Point 5140, Tiger Hill, and Point 4875.
Outcome: Complete eviction of intruders and restoration of the Line of Control by 26 July 1999, commemorated annually as Kargil Vijay Diwas. Four Param Vir Chakras and numerous Mahavir Chakras awarded.

Reference: From Surprise to Reckoning: Kargil Review Committee Report, New Delhi, 2000.

Period: Continuous since 1950
Operational Mandate: Deploy under the blue flag of the United Nations to monitor ceasefires, protect civilians, disarm combatants, and maintain stability in conflict zones worldwide.
Army's Role: India is historically one of the largest cumulative troop contributors to UN Peacekeeping, deploying over 260,000 personnel across more than 50 missions including Korea (Custodial Force India), Congo (ONUC), Gaza (UNEF), Cambodia (UNTAC), Lebanon (UNIFIL), and South Sudan (UNMISS). In 2007, India deployed the world's first all-female Formed Police Unit to Liberia.
Outcome: Globally recognized for neutrality, discipline, and community development. Over 175 Indian soldiers have made the supreme sacrifice under the UN flag, the highest of any troop-contributing country.

Reference: United Nations Department of Peace Operations (DPO); Integrated Defence Staff (IDS).
