Architecture of Command

Organisation and Structure of the Indian Army

A transparent, educational overview of the Army's governance, geographic commands, echelon hierarchy, and functional classification into combat arms and logistics services.

Constitutional Authority

Command Chain & Civilian Oversight

In strict accordance with the Constitution of India, the President of India is the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces. The political responsibility for national defence rests with the Union Cabinet, headed by the Prime Minister, with the Ministry of Defence (MoD) administering financial resources, policy formulation, and administrative integration.

Professional military command of the land forces is vested in the Chief of the Army Staff (COAS), a four-star General seated at Integrated Headquarters of Ministry of Defence (Army) in New Delhi. The COAS is assisted by the Vice Chief of the Army Staff (VCOAS), Deputy Chiefs of Army Staff (DCOAS), and Principal Staff Officers (Adjutant General, Quartermaster General, Master General of Sustenance, and Military Secretary).

The Indian Army operates under strict constitutional governance, civilian oversight, and meritocratic military command.
Theatre Architecture

The Seven Commands & Their Headquarters

The Indian Army is organised geographically into six operational commands and one functional training command, each commanded by a three-star Lieutenant General designated as General Officer Commanding-in-Chief (GOC-in-C).

Operational Command

Northern Command

Headquarters: Udhampur, Jammu & Kashmir
Area of Responsibility: Guarding northern frontiers along the Line of Control (LoC) in Jammu and Kashmir, and the Line of Actual Control (LAC) in Ladakh, including the Siachen Glacier.

Operational Command

Western Command

Headquarters: Chandimandir, Haryana
Area of Responsibility: Defending the international border across Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, and adjacent sectors, providing strike and defensive capability.

Operational Command

Eastern Command

Headquarters: Kolkata (Fort William), West Bengal
Area of Responsibility: Managing defence along the eastern Himalayan frontiers (Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh) and international boundaries with Bhutan, Myanmar, and Bangladesh.

Operational Command

Southern Command

Headquarters: Pune, Maharashtra
Area of Responsibility: The oldest command, responsible for peninsular India, the Rann of Kutch, and parts of Rajasthan and Gujarat along the western border.

Operational Command

Central Command

Headquarters: Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh
Area of Responsibility: Guarding the central sector of the northern border (Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh) and key training and logistics depots.

Operational Command

South Western Command

Headquarters: Jaipur, Rajasthan
Area of Responsibility: Established in 2005 to oversee the desert and semi-desert operational sectors along the Rajasthan and Haryana borders.

Functional Command

Army Training Command (ARTRAC) — HQ Shimla, Himachal Pradesh

Unlike the territorial combat commands, ARTRAC is responsible for formulating institutional war-fighting doctrines, updating joint operational concepts, and supervising curriculum standards across more than 30 Category 'A' and 'B' military training establishments nationwide.

Operational Echelons

The Field Hierarchy: From Command to Section

How formations break down from major strategic corps down to small tactical infantry fireteams.

Formation Level Typical Commander Composition & Role Estimated Personnel
Command Lieutenant General (GOC-in-C) Controls multiple Corps in a specific geographic theatre. Field Army level
Corps Lieutenant General (GOC) Highest operational field formation; categorized as "Holding" (defensive) or "Strike" (offensive) Corps. 60,000 – 85,000
Division Major General (GOC) Self-contained combined-arms formation; categorized into Mountain, Infantry, Armoured, or RAPID Divisions. 15,000 – 20,000
Brigade Brigadier Consists of 3 to 4 combat battalions/regiments plus dedicated artillery, engineer, and logistics elements. 3,000 – 4,500
Battalion / Regiment Colonel The fundamental fighting and tactical unit of Infantry (Battalion) or Armoured Corps / Artillery (Regiment). 800 – 1,000
Company / Battery Major / Captain Composed of three rifle platoons plus a support weapons section; led by a Company Commander. 120 – 150
Platoon Subedar / Naib Subedar (or Lieutenant) Consists of three sections of 10 soldiers each; fundamental maneuver element. 30 – 36
Section Havildar / Naik Smallest standard infantry tactical team, equipped with assault rifles, light machine gun, and support weapons. 10 soldiers
Functional Classification

Arms and Services of the Indian Army

Military components are classified according to their direct role on the battlefield.

Direct Combat

Combat Arms

Units that directly engage the enemy in close combat:

  • Infantry: Queen of the battlefield, closes with and destroys enemy forces across all terrains.
  • Armoured Corps: Provides heavy shock action, high-mobility firepower, and breakthrough capabilities.
  • Mechanised Infantry: Highly mobile infantry mounted on tracked Combat Infantry Fighting Vehicles (BMP-2).
Tactical Enablers

Combat Support Arms

Arms that directly support combat units with firepower and technical capabilities:

  • Regiment of Artillery: Long-range devastating firepower, precision strikes, and rocket systems.
  • Corps of Engineers (Bombay, Bengal, Madras Sappers): Mobility (bridging, mine clearing) and counter-mobility.
  • Corps of Signals: Secure encrypted telecommunications, cyber defence, and battlefield networks.
  • Army Air Defence (AAD): Protects ground formations from hostile aerial threats.
  • Army Aviation Corps: Observation, casualty evacuation, and combat reconnaissance.
Sustenance & Logistics

Services

Organisations ensuring continuous maintenance, nourishment, and administration:

  • Army Service Corps (ASC): Supply of rations, fuel, petroleum, and overland transportation.
  • Army Ordnance Corps (AOC): Ammunition, weapons, vehicles, and clothing supplies.
  • Corps of EME: Technical maintenance, repair, and recovery of combat systems.
  • Army Medical Corps (AMC): High-level field medicine, combat casualty care, and military hospitals.
  • Corps of Military Police (CMP): Traffic control, discipline, and security of lines of communication.
