Service to the People

Humanitarian Assistance & Disaster Relief (HADR)

Examining the statutory framework of “Aid to Civil Authorities” and documented disaster response missions that demonstrate the Indian Army's unwavering commitment to civilian life.

Legal & Institutional Protocol

Aid to Civil Authorities: The Guiding Doctrine

In the Indian constitutional system, disaster management is fundamentally the responsibility of civilian institutions—principally the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), State Disaster Management Authorities (SDMAs), and the specialised National Disaster Response Force (NDRF).

The Indian Army is requisitioned as the responder of last resort under the statutory provisions of “Aid to Civil Authorities” when civilian infrastructure is overwhelmed by sudden natural calamities such as catastrophic earthquakes, cloudburst floods, or massive landslides.

Equipped with self-contained field mobility, military engineers (Sappers), combat bridging assets, heavy earthmoving plants, field medical hospitals, and rotary aviation, the Army can establish operational relief bridges and medical triage lines within hours in inaccessible terrain.

Indian Army Sappers establishing an emergency ropeway rescue corridor across a swollen mountain river during disaster relief operations.
Documented Relief Missions

Historical Case Studies of Disaster Response

Factual summaries of verified major rescue and relief operations launched across India over the past two decades.

2001 · Gujarat

Bhuj Earthquake Relief

Following the catastrophic 7.7 magnitude earthquake in Kutch on 26 January 2001, the Indian Army's Southern Command mobilised over 20,000 personnel. Army Medical Corps (AMC) deployed 200-bed field hospitals within 48 hours to perform emergency surgeries. Military engineers cleared collapsed debris, re-established telecommunication links, and restored water purification plants in Bhuj, Anjar, and Bachau.

Source: Ministry of Defence Annual Report 2001–2002; National Disaster Management Division.
2004 · Southern Coast & Andamans

Operation Sea Waves & Operation Madad

Following the 26 December 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami, the Army mobilised engineering and medical columns across the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and coastal Tamil Nadu. Troops re-established severed communications, erected relief camps, and restored coastal roads, while also providing humanitarian assistance to Sri Lanka (Operation Rainbow) and the Maldives (Operation Castor).

Source: Ministry of External Affairs & Integrated Defence Staff (IDS) Official Reports.
June 2013 · Uttarakhand

Operation Surya Hope (Uttarakhand Floods)

When devastating cloudbursts and flash floods struck Kedarnath and the Alaknanda/Mandakini river valleys in June 2013, the Indian Army launched Operation Surya Hope under Central Command. Over 8,000 soldiers carved out emergency footpaths across sheer landslides, launched 14 temporary bridges, and established satellite medical triage posts, ensuring the evacuation of thousands of stranded pilgrims from high-altitude valleys.

Source: Central Command, Indian Army, Operation Surya Hope Official Chronicle, 2013.
September 2014 · Jammu & Kashmir

Operation Megh Rahat (Kashmir Floods)

Historic rainfall in September 2014 caused catastrophic flooding of the Jhelum River, inundating Srinagar city and south Kashmir. The Army deployed 30,000 personnel, over 200 motorized boats, and aviation assets to rescue marooned hospital patients and residents, distribute millions of liters of clean drinking water, and restore breached embankments.

Source: Press Information Bureau (PIB) Defence Wing, September 2014 Releases.
August 2018 · Kerala

Operation Sahyog (Kerala Floods)

In August 2018, unprecedented monsoon downpours triggered widespread flooding and landslides across Kerala. Southern Command deployed engineering columns, composite flood relief teams, and medical units to Wayanad, Idukki, Ernakulam, and Alappuzha. Soldiers constructed improvisational bridges from local fallen timber to reconnect cut-off hill villages.

Source: Headquarters Southern Command, Report on Operation Sahyog, 2018.
July – August 2024 · Kerala

Chooralmala Bailey Bridge at Wayanad

Following massive landslides in Meppadi, Wayanad in July 2024, the Indian Army's Madras Sappers (Madras Engineer Group) constructed a 190-foot Bailey Bridge connecting Chooralmala to Mundakkai within a record 31 hours amidst challenging weather. This vital 24-ton capacity bridge allowed heavy excavators and rescue teams to reach the affected disaster zone.

Source: Ministry of Defence / PIB Press Release, 1 August 2024.
