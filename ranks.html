Hierarchy & Protocol

Ranks and Insignia of the Indian Army

A definitive reference covering all three personnel tiers: Commissioned Officers, Junior Commissioned Officers (JCOs), and Other Ranks.

Understanding the Tri-Service Distinction

Indian Army ranks use a distinctive nomenclature derived from land warfare traditions. They are completely separate from the Indian Navy (e.g. Sub Lieutenant, Commander, Captain, Admiral) and the Indian Air Force (e.g. Flying Officer, Wing Commander, Group Captain, Air Chief Marshal).

The Three Tier System

The Indian Army's human architecture is organized into three clearly defined statutory categories under the Army Act, 1950:

  • Commissioned Officers: Bear the President of India's Commission. They provide tactical, operational, and strategic leadership.
  • Junior Commissioned Officers (JCOs): A unique institution inherited and refined in South Asian armies. Granted a gazetted commission by the President, JCOs bridge senior commanders with enlisted troops.
  • Non-Commissioned Officers & Other Ranks (ORs): Enlisted soldiers (Jawans) and section leaders who execute missions directly on the ground.
Newly commissioned Lieutenants receive their rank stars at the Passing Out Parade of the Indian Military Academy and Officers Training Academy.
Tier 1: Strategic & Tactical Leadership

Commissioned Officer Ranks

Listed in descending order from highest to entry-level commission.

Rank Title Insignia Elements Grade / Stars Standard Command Appointment
Field Marshal National Emblem over crossed baton and saber in a lotus blossom wreath 5-Star (Honorary / Wartime) Conferred only twice in Indian history: to Field Marshal Sam Manekshaw (1973) and Field Marshal K.M. Cariappa (1986). A lifetime rank.
General National Emblem over crossed sword and baton, with one five-pointed star 4-Star Chief of the Army Staff (COAS) and Chief of Defence Staff (when held by an Army officer).
Lieutenant General National Emblem over crossed sword and baton 3-Star Army Commander (GOC-in-C), Corps Commander (GOC), Vice Chief of Army Staff (VCOAS), Principal Staff Officers.
Major General One five-pointed star over crossed sword and baton 2-Star Division Commander (GOC), Chief of Staff at Corps HQ, Inspector General.
Brigadier National Emblem over three five-pointed stars in a triangle 1-Star Brigade Commander, Sub-Area Commander, Military Attaché.
Colonel National Emblem over two five-pointed stars Senior Selection Battalion Commanding Officer (CO) of an Infantry Battalion or Artillery / Armoured Regiment.
Lieutenant Colonel National Emblem over one five-pointed star Time-Scale / Selection Battalion Second-in-Command (2IC), Staff Officer at Brigade or Division HQ.
Major National Emblem (Ashoka Lion Capital) Time-Scale Company Commander, Squadron Commander, Battery Commander.
Captain Three five-pointed stars Time-Scale Company Second-in-Command, Adjutant, Quartermaster.
Lieutenant Two five-pointed stars Entry Rank Platoon Commander upon passing out of IMA Dehradun or OTA Chennai.
Tiers 2 & 3: Operational Backbone

Junior Commissioned Officers & Other Ranks

The backbone of the Indian Army's unit discipline, combat cohesion, and regimental ethos.

Tier 2: Junior Commissioned Officers

JCO Hierarchy

JCOs are promoted from the ranks based on merit, competitive selection, and exemplary service. They wear special red-and-gold shoulder tape beneath their rank badges:

Insignia: Gold Ashoka Lion Emblem with a red-and-gold stripe beneath.
Role: The senior-most JCO in a battalion/regiment. Acts as the principal confidential advisor to the Commanding Officer (CO) on troop welfare, traditions, and unit discipline.
Insignia: Two gold five-pointed stars with red-and-gold stripe beneath.
Role: Typically acts as Platoon Commander or Company Second-in-Command.
Insignia: One gold five-pointed star with red-and-gold stripe beneath.
Role: First rank in the JCO cadre. Commands a rifle platoon or specialized technical section.
Tier 3: Non-Commissioned Officers & Enlisted

Non-Commissioned Officers (NCOs) & Soldiers

Enlisted combatants who form the fighting strength of field units:

Insignia: Three chevrons worn on the right sleeve.
Role: Commands a rifle section (10 soldiers) or acts as Company Havildar Major (CHM).
Insignia: Two chevrons on the right sleeve.
Role: Section Second-in-Command.
Insignia: One chevron on the right sleeve.
Role: Fire team leader.
Insignia: Plain regimental shoulder badge.
Designations: Sepoy (Infantry), Sowar (Armoured), Gunner (Artillery), Sapper (Engineers), Signalman (Signals), Rifleman (Rifles regiments).
